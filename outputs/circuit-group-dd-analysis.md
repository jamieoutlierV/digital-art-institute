# Due Diligence Analysis: The Circuit Group, Inc.

**Prepared:** 8 August 2026 (from public-domain sources)
**Subject:** The Circuit Group, Inc. — Los Angeles / Miami / New York / London. Electronic-music artist management, IP and services collective; parent of management firms Seven20 and AYITA.

> **Disambiguation:** "Circuit Group" here refers to *The Circuit Group, Inc.* (US/UK, founded 2023, electronic music management + IP). It is unrelated to circuit-bending art practice or to any Amsterdam events entity. All findings below are from public reporting; the company is private and discloses no financials.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

The Circuit Group is a late-2023 merger of two of electronic music's most powerful management firms — Seven20 (Dean & Jessica Wilson; deadmau5) and AYITA (Brett Fischer, David Gray, Harvey Tadman; FISHER, Chris Lake) — built around a distinctive and contentious thesis: **the management company takes equity (up to 50%) in its clients' IP portfolios** and then builds the service stack (labels, publishing, events, brand ventures, catalog capital) to appreciate that IP.

In three years it has assembled a full-stack music company: label services (2024), publishing with Kobalt administration (2024), UK and artist-development JVs (2024–25), boutique publisher Red Wire (Oct 2025), a **nine-figure catalog fund, Circuit Capital, with access to $500m+ of capital from Create Music Group** (Nov 2025), and indie services platform Beat Switch (public Feb 2026, quietly operating ~24 months prior).

**Overall read:** a credible, well-executed consolidation play on the electronic-music value chain, with genuine flywheel logic — but with material governance/conflict-of-interest questions (manager-as-co-owner of client IP; the CEO's client's catalog sold to the entity now financing the group's fund), heavy key-person and genre concentration, and a financing structure whose "$500m" headline is *access to* a backer's capital, not a committed blind-pool fund. Counterparty risk at Create Music Group (active litigation and past reporting on YouTube royalty-claiming practices) deserves scrutiny.

## 3. Business Model

### Core thesis
Traditional management economics (commission on artist income) are replaced/augmented by an **equity model in artist IP**: Circuit seeks up to 50% ownership of clients' IP portfolios and in exchange invests capital and operational infrastructure to grow the value of that IP across verticals — recordings, publishing, live/events, brands, merchandise, technology. The manager stops being an agent-for-fee and becomes a **co-owner and operator of artist businesses**.

### Revenue streams (assembled 2023–26)
1. **Artist management commissions** (Seven20, AYITA + JV rosters via CtrlFrk, Buena/Ahearn, ALT:Vision)
2. **IP equity participation** — shared ownership in client catalogs, labels and brands
3. **Label services** — A&R, marketing, distribution support for owned/affiliated labels
4. **Publishing** — Circuit Publishing / Red Wire Publishing; admin economics via Kobalt; 300+ copyrights; clients incl. FISHER, Chris Lake, Sammy Virji, Diplo, bbyclose
5. **Beat Switch** — white-label indie artist/label services: distribution, rights management, royalty accounting, publishing admin, sync, DSP pitching, DJ/radio promo, consultancy, and "Web3 platform services"; 20+ labels incl. Black Book Records (Chris Lake), Catch & Release (FISHER), COS Recordings and others
6. **Circuit Capital** — catalog and music-IP acquisition vehicle (deal economics/carry structure undisclosed); also finances Circuit-managed artists' back-catalog buys and futures deals
7. **Events/brand IP** — client-side event brands (e.g. Under Construction — FISHER × Chris Lake B2B arena/festival brand) and event/technology services traded into the Defected partnership

### Flywheel logic
Management relationships originate IP → group takes equity → in-house services (labels, publishing, events, Beat Switch) raise the IP's cash flows → Circuit Capital provides exit/liquidity or acquisition capital at the top → proceeds recycle into the roster. Each vertical feeds deal flow to the others. It is a genuinely integrated model rather than a holding-company roll-up — closer to a "Ithaca Holdings for dance music" than to a festival consolidator.

## 5. Financing

### The Circuit Group itself
- **No disclosed outside equity.** No venture/PE round in The Circuit Group, Inc. appears in public records; the company appears principal-funded from the founders' management businesses. Revenue, profitability, and cap table are not public. (A data room request would need: inter-company structure Seven20/AYITA/Circuit; IP-equity contract templates; artist consent/counsel representations.)

### Circuit Capital (Nov 2025)
- Headline: **"access to more than $500 million"** in backing from **Create Music Group (CMG)** to acquire catalogs, record labels, publishers and music-driven ventures, plus back-catalog and futures funding for Circuit-managed artists.
- **Structure caveat:** this is reported as *access to* CMG capital, not a closed, committed blind-pool fund with named LPs. Deployment pace, discretion, approval rights, and economics between Circuit and CMG are undisclosed. The headline number should be treated as a ceiling on availability, not AUM.

### Create Music Group (the backer) — counterparty profile
- LA-based music/tech company; became a unicorn in 2024 via a **$165m round led by Flexpoint Ford (~$1bn valuation)**; completed a **$450m equity + debt raise at a $2.2bn valuation (March 2026)**.
- On an electronic-catalog acquisition run: **deadmau5/mau5trap catalog (~$55m, Mar 2025)**, **!K7 (Apr 2025)**, and others.
- **Litigation/reputation flags:** sued by Cinq Music (2022) over YouTube royalty claims; sued by **Artist Partner Group (Jan 2025)** alleging willful copyright infringement, "bogus" artist contracts and artist poaching; Billboard reporting (2022) described executives alleging CMG claimed YouTube royalties it had no rights to (CMG attributed a cited Louis Armstrong case to error). None of this is adjudicated wrongdoing, but it is a live diligence item on the group's principal financing partner.

### Related-party web (the key governance finding)
Dean Wilson is (a) deadmau5's manager, (b) CEO of The Circuit Group, which held/holds catalog interests alongside deadmau5, and (c) the sale of deadmau5's catalog went to CMG — (d) the same company now providing Circuit Capital's $500m backing, and (e) CMG holds a JV with deadmau5/mau5trap for future releases. Each leg may be individually defensible and artist-approved, but the circularity means **valuation discipline and fiduciary separation between "manager," "buyer," and "financier" is the single most important thing to verify** in any formal process.

## 6. Risk Assessment

| Risk | Severity | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Manager/owner conflict of interest | **High** | Taking 50% of client IP while acting as fiduciary manager is the model's core tension; artist-side counsel quality and consent mechanics are unverified publicly |
| Related-party transactions with CMG | **High** | Manager on multiple sides of catalog sales and fund financing; pricing integrity unverifiable from outside |
| Counterparty (CMG) litigation/reputation | **Medium-High** | APG and Cinq suits pending/settled; YouTube royalty-claiming reporting; CMG solvency itself looks strong post-$450m raise |
| "Access to $500m" ≠ committed capital | **Medium** | Deployment discretion and economics undisclosed; headline may overstate firepower |
| Key-person concentration | **High** | Dean Wilson, Brett Fischer; loss of any of the three anchor artists (deadmau5, FISHER, Chris Lake) would materially impair the flywheel |
| Genre/cycle concentration | **Medium** | Tech-house/dance touring economy near cycle highs; catalog values in electronic music are less seasoned than pop/rock in secondary markets |
| Artist churn | **Medium** | Management contracts are short; IP equity creates lock-in but also potential for acrimonious disputes if relationships sour |
| Opacity | **Medium** | No public financials at all; every revenue figure would need direct disclosure |

## 8. Addendum: Deal Economics & Peer Structures

### What has actually been paid (disclosed figures)

| Deal | Date | Value | Structure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Circuit founding IP stakes ("with deadmau5 and more") | Oct 2023 | Undisclosed | Up to 50% equity in client IP portfolios in exchange for capital + services |
| Circuit electronic catalog acquisitions | Sep 2025 | Undisclosed | Direct catalog purchases, pre-Circuit Capital |
| deadmau5 + mau5trap → Create Music Group | Mar 2025 | ~$55m | Full buyout of masters + publishing (4,000+ tracks) **plus JV for future releases** |
| !K7 Music → Create Music Group | Apr 2025 | Undisclosed | Whole-company acquisition |
| Circuit Capital backing (CMG) | Nov 2025 | Access to $500m+ | Backer balance-sheet access, not a closed blind-pool fund |
| CMG capitalisation | 2024–Mar 2026 | $165m at $1bn; $450m (equity+debt) at $2.2bn | Flexpoint Ford-led |

### Recurring deal-structure templates
1. **Management + IP equity ("the 50% model")** — Circuit's founding structure; consideration mix undisclosed.
2. **Buyout + futures JV** — deadmau5/CMG template; mirrors Warner–David Guetta (~$100m catalog + go-forward deal). Dominant structure for still-active electronic artists.
3. **Futures funding / advances** — Circuit Capital mandate includes advancing capital against managed artists' future royalties (pricing discipline is the diligence question when manager = financier).
4. **Strategic services swap** — Defected partnership: reciprocal infrastructure, no disclosed cash.

### Peer benchmarks
- **Pophouse** (Stockholm): Swedish House Mafia (full masters+publishing incl. writer's shares + brand JV, 2022); Avicii (75% stake, 2022); KISS (~$300m incl. name/likeness, 2024); Tina Turner (majority, 2026). Buy IP → build experiences.
- **BEAT Music Fund** (Armada, backed by Pinnacle Financial Partners, 2023): ~$100m intended deployment; bought KMS/Kevin Saunderson, ARTY, King Street Sounds, Chocolate Puma, Markus Schulz, Robbie Rivera, Tiga, Jax Jones. **Closest structural comp to Circuit Capital** — genre fund attached to a label machine.
- **Vine Alternative Investments**: Calvin Harris publishing (~$90–110m, 2020) — financial buyer, publishing only.
- **HYBE–Ithaca Holdings** ($1.05bn, 2021): the manager-equity holdco exit precedent; ~$161m in shares distributed to 39 insiders including ~$11m each to Bieber and Grande.
- **Hipgnosis → Blackstone**: the cautionary blind-pool comp; price-setter of the low-rate era, since repriced.

### Market pricing context
Shot Tower Capital: publishing deals >$20m EV averaged **16.1× NPS in 2024** (16.7× 2023); "iconic" deals >$200m ~14.2× NLS. Multiples moderated from the 2021 peak. Electronic catalogs generally clear below blended averages; strategic synergy buyers (CMG, BEAT), not yield funds, set the marginal price. The $55m deadmau5 deal has no published revenue base, so no implied multiple can be computed.
